GaussianBench

Physics-fidelity evaluation for Gaussian scene representations

Chukwudalu Dumebi-KachikwuSTAR Labs, University of Maryland

Paper arXiv Code BibTeX

Can a system do more than make physics look right?

3D Gaussian Splatting is moving from static reconstruction toward systems meant to predict how scenes change under interaction. Those systems are usually judged by rendered motion or a method-specific task, yet a rollout can look plausible while conserving momentum incorrectly, transmitting force wrongly across a material boundary, or never reaching the deformation it was asked for. GaussianBench tests those claims directly, from exported physical state.

  1. 1Lock the test

    Each versioned scene fixes its setup, analytical or measured reference, regime of validity and tolerance before any entrant runs.

  2. 2Run native physics

    A validated thin adapter hands the same scene to the released system, pinned to a commit, and exports the state it actually produced.

  3. 3Grade and diagnose

    Pure-function scorers, unit-tested on synthetic trajectories, return a verdict and point to the physical contract that failed.

Pass
The applicable test ran and met its reference within tolerance.
Fail
The test ran and missed its reference: a genuine physical failure.
Invalid
The prescribed regime was never realized, so the comparison is not valid.
NA
The release does not expose the required state or capability.

Test the motion, the relationships, and the representation.

Each protocol asks a different question of the same exported state, so a failure can be attributed to wrong motion, wrong constitutive response, wrong Gaussian-state transport, or an invalid regime.

ADynamics

Does the object obey basic physics over time?

Momentum conservation, beam frequency, stress-wave speed, Poisson contraction, melt-front motion and free fall, each against a known solution.

A1 conservation · A2 Euler–Bernoulli · A3 wave speed · A4 Neumann–Stefan · A5 Poisson · A6 free fall

BRelationships

Does it preserve relationships and respond to change?

A bimaterial bar checks regional strains and interface traction. Interventions change one input (gravity, temperature, or material assignment) and compare against the predicted counterfactual.

B1 series bar · thermal intervention · material repainting

CGaussian state

Does the representation itself stay physically correct?

A position-fitted deformation checks whether the requested motion happened; Σ′ = FΣ0F⊤ checks whether covariance followed it. Health, rendering and native-triangle checks complete the tier.

C1 affine · C2 shear and health · C3 render · C4 captured beam · C5–C8 triangle state

MMeasured material

Does the constitutive law match real material?

Simulated stress–strain is compared with an independent three-term Yeoh fit to ASTM D412 natural-rubber measurements, up to 150% engineering strain.

Uniform success does not guarantee interface fidelity.

OmniPhysGS and PhysDreamer pass homogeneous beam, wave and Poisson probes, yet on the B1 bimaterial bar their largest regional-strain error reaches 105.0% and 105.6%, with a 55% traction mismatch across the interface.

45.3% vs 0.075%

Motion and Gaussian transport are separable.

Under unit shear, PhysGaussian and Physics3D miss the requested deformation by 45.3%, so the scene is INVALID, while covariance is still transported correctly for the deformation they did produce.

The failures are not threshold accidents.

Doubling every verdict-bearing error budget changes zero external verdicts. Validity gates stay frozen, so relaxing a tolerance cannot turn an unrealized experiment into a pass.

GaussianFlesh exercises the complete benchmark.

Every Gaussian is both a rendering primitive and a continuum material point with persistent identity. A shared-grid UL-MLS-MPM solver dispatches each particle’s own stress law, so one connected object can hold stiff, soft and plastic regions that exchange momentum through the same grid.

  • Covariance push-forward Σp = FpΣ0,pFp⊤
  • Corotated, J2-plastic metal, Drucker–Prager clay and molten branches
  • Grid heat diffusion, µ(T) softening and latent-heat melting

GaussianFlesh passes 11 of its 12 applicable scenes; its only miss is the strict single-precision A1a momentum tier. GaussianBench and its cross-system diagnoses are the contribution; GaussianFlesh shows one implementation of the contract.

Visual plausibility is not physical correctness.

As Gaussian splatting moves toward interactive scenes and world models, believable pixels are not enough. GaussianBench makes behavior under new forces, contacts, materials and temperatures measurable, attributable and reproducible, and tells us which part failed.
